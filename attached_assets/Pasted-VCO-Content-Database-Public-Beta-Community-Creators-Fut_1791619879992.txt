VCO Content Database – Public Beta, Community Creators & Future Monetization

Objective

Prepare the existing VCO Content Database for a simple, user-friendly public beta.

The platform has two distinct purposes:

Primary purpose: Make official VCO photos, videos, events and historical media easily accessible, searchable and downloadable.

Secondary purpose: Allow community photographers and Creators to publish their own photo galleries, initially free of charge.

In Phase 2, the platform should offer opportunities for Creators to monetize their content.

Important implementation principle: Inspect first, reuse existing functionality, then make only the necessary changes.

Do not rebuild working features or unnecessarily expand the application.

⸻

1. Audit the Existing Implementation First

Before making changes, inspect the current code and database structures for:

* User registration and authentication
* Email verification
* Creator applications, approval and permissions
* Creator dashboards
* Gallery and event management
* Photo and video uploads
* Storage quotas and upload restrictions
* AI tagging and metadata
* Downloads and access permissions
* Public gallery visibility
* Admin moderation and suspension
* Credit balances and premium content
* Stripe integration and payment infrastructure
* Creator payouts or revenue-sharing structures, if present

For each relevant component, determine whether it is:

* Already implemented and usable
* Implemented but inactive
* Partially implemented
* Missing

Reuse existing functionality wherever possible.

Do not treat older documentation as authoritative if it conflicts with the actual implementation.

Do not delete or migrate existing payment-related data merely because monetization is disabled during the beta.

2. Product Positioning – VCO First

The VCO Content Database is primarily the official media library for VCO.

The homepage and main navigation should prioritize:

* Official VCO media
* Events and racing series
* Photos and videos
* Historical content
* Search and discovery

Community Creator galleries complement the official content.

They should be easy to discover without dominating the homepage.

Avoid positioning the platform primarily as a photo marketplace or Creator monetization service.

Reuse the existing visual design and navigation structure.

Do not redesign the entire website.

3. Open Creator Registration

Community members should be able to become Creators without manual administrator approval.

Preferred flow:

1. User registers.
2. User verifies their email address.
3. User activates Creator access.
4. User creates a gallery.
5. User uploads photos.
6. Gallery becomes publicly accessible.

Reuse the existing registration and Creator account infrastructure.

Remove the manual approval requirement from the normal onboarding flow.

Important:

* Do not automatically reactivate suspended or explicitly rejected accounts.
* Preserve administrator permissions and account blocking.
* Creators may only manage their own content.
* Official VCO content remains protected.
* Existing Creator accounts and galleries must continue working.
* Do not introduce an additional mandatory onboarding wizard.

If email verification is not currently implemented, identify the smallest secure way to enable it before public publishing.

4. VCO Creator Free – Initial Limits

Introduce or adjust the free Community Creator tier to the following limits:

Resource	Free Limit
Total storage	500 MB
Galleries	5
Photos	1,000
Maximum file size per photo	5 MB
Video uploads	Not allowed
AI tagging	Included, subject to fair-use safeguards
Creator monetization	Not available in Phase 1

Use decimal units:

* 500 MB = 500,000,000 bytes
* 5 MB = 5,000,000 bytes

Quota enforcement

Enforce limits server-side.

Include:

* Uploaded photos
* Stored Creator reference documents
* Other persistent Creator-owned files where applicable

Avoid double-counting shared files or temporary processing artifacts.

Concurrent uploads must not bypass quotas.

Gallery and photo counts must be enforced reliably.

Regular Community Creators must not be able to bypass restrictions through alternative upload endpoints.

Official VCO exception

These Community Creator limits must not restrict:

* Official VCO media
* Authorized administrator uploads
* Existing official video functionality

Preserve existing role-based permissions.

5. Creator Dashboard & Usability

Keep the Creator dashboard simple.

Show a compact usage overview:

* Storage used / 500 MB
* Galleries used / 5
* Photos used / 1,000

Example:

“245 MB of 500 MB used”

Provide clear messages when limits are reached.

Examples:

* “Your storage is full.”
* “You have reached the maximum of 5 galleries.”
* “Images must be smaller than 5 MB.”
* “Video uploads are not available for Community Creators.”

Respect the existing application language and localization.

Do not expose technical storage units, backend errors or internal quota calculations to users.

Do not add complex billing, upgrade or subscription interfaces.

The normal Creator workflow should remain:

Create gallery → Upload photos → Edit if needed → Publish.

Avoid unnecessary confirmation dialogs.

6. Creator Photo Uploads

Community Creators may upload photos only.

Accepted formats should follow the existing supported image formats, subject to secure file validation.

Maximum upload size: 5 MB per image.

Keep:

* Existing upload functionality
* Automatic AI tagging
* Manual tags and captions
* Event/gallery assignment
* Background processing
* Existing download functionality

Do not require additional steps for AI analysis.

Video uploads must be rejected server-side for regular Community Creators, including through direct API requests.

Official VCO video uploads remain available.

Do not remove existing video processing or AI video tagging functionality.

7. AI Tagging & Cost Protection

Keep automatic AI tagging available to Community Creators.

Reuse the existing AI analysis infrastructure.

Apply reasonable server-side safeguards:

* Prevent unnecessary repeated analysis of unchanged media.
* Rate-limit manual reanalysis.
* Respect existing background processing concurrency limits.
* Prevent a single Creator from monopolizing the shared AI queue.
* Preserve manually edited tags and metadata.
* Avoid duplicate provider requests where completed results can be reused.

Do not introduce visible AI credits or complex usage controls during Phase 1.

Keep safeguards configurable for future growth.

Do not redesign the AI tagging system as part of this task.

8. Public Galleries & Basic Moderation

Creator galleries should be publicly accessible without manual editorial approval after the Creator account has been activated.

Preserve or implement minimal safeguards:

* Verified email before public publishing
* Existing authentication and authorization
* Ownership checks
* Server-side file validation
* Reasonable upload and request rate limits
* Admin ability to hide or unpublish galleries
* Admin ability to suspend Creator accounts
* Simple content reporting where already supported or achievable with minimal changes

Do not introduce a complex editorial approval workflow.

Preserve existing public/private visibility controls if available.

Ensure newly uploaded content does not bypass any existing moderation or account restrictions.

9. Phase 2 – Creator Monetization

Creator monetization is a planned future development phase.

Potential features include:

* Paid photo downloads
* Premium galleries
* Creator revenue sharing
* Creator payouts
* Paid storage extensions
* Additional gallery capacity
* Subscription-based Creator tiers

The exact commercial model is not finalized.

Preserve existing infrastructure

If any of the following already exist, retain them:

* Stripe integration
* Payment service configuration
* Credit balances
* Credit transaction records
* Premium media flags
* Checkout infrastructure
* Payment webhooks
* Creator payout structures
* Revenue-sharing logic
* Subscription-related database fields

Do not delete these components simply because they are not currently active.

Do not perform destructive database migrations.

Do not remove existing historical payment or credit records.

Keep future monetization functionality technically available for Phase 2, but inactive for public beta users.

Phase 1 restrictions

During the public beta:

* No active Credit purchases
* No active Stripe checkout for regular users
* No Creator payouts
* No paid subscriptions
* No public pricing or upgrade flows
* No automatic conversion of premium content into free content

Preserve the existing premium access restrictions.

Do not accidentally make previously premium content freely downloadable.

Disable or hide inactive commercial controls in the public interface rather than removing their underlying implementation.

Future compatibility

Where practical, keep Creator limits configurable so paid tiers can be introduced later.

Do not implement a new billing abstraction, pricing model or subscription engine now.

10. Communicating Future Monetization

The platform should mention the planned opportunity for Creators to earn money from their content in a suitable, secondary location.

Suggested English copy:

“Share your racing photography with the community. We’re planning future features that will allow Creators to earn money from their content.”

Suggested German copy:

“Teile deine Motorsport-Fotografie mit der Community. Für eine spätere Ausbaustufe planen wir Funktionen, mit denen Creator Einnahmen aus ihren Inhalten erzielen können.”

Use this messaging in the Creator information or onboarding area.

Do not make monetization a primary homepage message.

Do not promise specific launch dates, earnings, commissions or commercial terms.

Do not advertise monetization as an already available feature.

11. Existing Users & Data

Preserve:

* Existing user accounts
* Existing approved Creator accounts
* Existing galleries
* Existing uploaded media
* Existing download records
* Existing credits and payment records
* Existing premium content restrictions

Handle existing Creators whose usage exceeds the new free limits safely.

Do not delete their content.

Do not automatically remove galleries or files.

If an existing Creator exceeds the new quota, preserve existing content and restrict only additional uploads or gallery creation until usage is within the applicable limit or an administrator grants an exception.

Do not automatically activate suspended accounts.

Avoid unnecessary database migrations.

12. Security & Ownership

Maintain strict separation between:

* Regular users
* Community Creators
* Official VCO content managers
* Administrators

Creators must not modify or delete other Creators’ content.

Community quotas must not affect official VCO operations.

Preserve existing authentication, authorization, download permissions and storage access controls.

Do not introduce new public access to private files.

13. Focused Validation

Test only the relevant workflows.

Registration

* New user registration
* Email verification
* Creator activation without manual approval
* Suspended accounts remain blocked

Uploads

* Valid photo upload
* Image larger than 5 MB rejected
* Creator video upload rejected
* Official VCO video upload still works

Quotas

* 500 MB storage limit
* 5-gallery limit
* 1,000-photo limit
* Concurrent upload protection
* Correct usage display

Permissions

* Creator can manage own galleries
* Creator cannot manage another Creator’s content
* Official VCO content remains protected
* Admin moderation continues working

Payments

* Stripe checkout remains inactive for public beta
* Credits cannot be purchased
* Premium content remains protected
* Existing payment-related records remain intact

Keep tests proportionate.

Do not perform an extensive application-wide regression campaign unless changes reveal a specific risk.

14. Implementation Rules

* Inspect the existing implementation before coding.
* Reuse working components.
* Make focused changes.
* Preserve existing database records.
* Avoid unnecessary architectural changes.
* Do not create a separate Creator application.
* Do not introduce new payment services.
* Do not rebuild Stripe or Credits.
* Do not redesign the entire frontend.
* Do not modify production deployment configuration.
* Do not publish automatically.

If a requirement conflicts with existing functionality or would require substantial architectural changes, explain the issue before expanding scope.

15. Final Report

After implementation, provide a concise report:

1. What was already implemented
2. What was changed
3. Which Creator limits are now enforced
4. How the open Creator registration works
5. What was preserved for Phase 2 monetization
6. Which usability improvements were made
7. Test results
8. Remaining issues or decisions

Primary Success Criteria

For VCO users:

The platform remains a fast, intuitive and searchable media database focused on official VCO content.

For Community Creators:

A new user can register, activate Creator access, create a gallery and upload photos within minutes, without manual approval or payment.

For future development:

Existing Stripe, Credit, Premium and payment-related infrastructure remains safely preserved for Phase 2, without exposing inactive commercial functionality during the public beta.

Prioritize simplicity, reliability, low operating costs and reuse of the existing architecture.
