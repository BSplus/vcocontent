⸻

1. Fundamental Principle: Preserve, Reuse, Optimize

The VCO Content Database is an existing application with substantial working functionality.

Do not rebuild components merely to create a theoretically cleaner architecture.

The preferred engineering approach is:

1. REUSE working functionality.
2. EXTEND existing components where necessary.
3. REFACTOR selectively when there is a demonstrable benefit.
4. REPLACE only when the existing implementation cannot reasonably meet the requirements.

For every proposed architectural change, evaluate:

* Performance and response times.
* Infrastructure and operating costs.
* Development effort.
* Maintainability.
* Operational complexity.
* Reliability.
* Security.
* Scalability.
* Compatibility with existing workflows.
* Regression risk.

For every proposed replacement, document:

* Current limitation.
* Proposed solution.
* Measurable or clearly explainable benefit.
* Implementation effort.
* Operational implications.
* Migration requirements.
* Regression risk.
* Why a smaller modification is insufficient.

Default decision: REUSE or EXTEND.

Avoid unnecessary microservices, databases, storage systems, processing services and third-party dependencies.

Prefer the existing PostgreSQL, Cloudflare R2 and application infrastructure wherever appropriate.

The goal is not architectural perfection.

The goal is the fastest, safest, most cost-efficient and most maintainable path to the required product.

2. Two Operating Models — One Shared Platform

This is a fundamental product requirement.

The VCO Content Database must continue to support:

A. Official VCO-managed events, galleries and media

B. External Creator-owned events, galleries and media

Both operating models must use the same underlying technology wherever practical.

Do not build two separate applications or duplicate processing pipelines.

2.1 Shared Functionality

Both official VCO content and external Creator content must support:

* Events and galleries.
* JPEG photographs.
* Optional RAW variants.
* Video clips.
* AI tagging.
* Driver and vehicle identification.
* Event and reference matching.
* AI captioning.
* Manual metadata corrections.
* IPTC, EXIF and XMP workflows.
* Media search and discovery.
* Preview generation.
* Protected downloads.
* Licensing.
* Marketplace sales where enabled.
* Cloudflare R2 storage.
* Integrity and security controls.

The existing AI pipeline must serve both operating models.

2.2 Official VCO Operating Model

Official VCO events and galleries:

1. Continue to function as they do today.
2. Are managed by authorized VCO administrators.
3. Do not require a Creator subscription.
4. Are not subject to Free, Pro, Studio or Archive storage quotas.
5. Are not blocked by Creator subscription storage limits.
6. Can contain JPEG, RAW and video assets.
7. Use the same AI tagging, matching and captioning technology.
8. Can contain free or paid assets.
9. Can use VCO-defined licences and prices.
10. Do not incur an internal Creator commission.
11. Retain their existing event, gallery and media relationships.
12. Remain available independently of external Creator subscription features.

Official VCO storage is operationally unlimited with respect to Creator subscription quotas.

This does not mean unlimited physical infrastructure resources.

Track actual storage consumption, processing usage and costs.

Apply technical safety limits, upload validation and abuse protection.

However, do not introduce subscription-based upload restrictions for official VCO galleries.

Official VCO content must not need an artificial Creator plan to bypass quota enforcement.

2.3 External Creator Operating Model

External Creators:

* Manage their own galleries and media.
* Use the same underlying asset and AI technology.
* Have subscription-based storage allowances.
* May purchase additional storage.
* Control their own Marketplace prices.
* Receive earnings from eligible sales.
* Pay the applicable Marketplace commission.
* Manage storage within their purchased allowance.

2.4 Ownership Must Be Explicit

The system must reliably distinguish:

* Official VCO-owned or VCO-managed content.
* External Creator-owned content.

Do not determine ownership merely from the identity of the uploader.

An administrator uploading into an external Creator gallery consumes that Creator’s storage allowance.

An administrator uploading into an official VCO gallery uses the official VCO storage allocation and is not subject to Creator subscription quotas.

Preserve existing official-event ownership conventions, including nullable Creator ownership where currently used, unless a targeted change is justified.

Avoid ambiguous ownership states.

2.5 Shared Marketplace Infrastructure

Official VCO assets may participate in the Marketplace.

For VCO-owned assets:

* VCO controls pricing.
* VCO controls licensing.
* VCO receives the applicable sales proceeds.
* No internal Creator commission is deducted.

Use the same purchasing, entitlement and delivery infrastructure as external Creator assets.

Do not create a separate VCO checkout system.

5. Unified Logical Asset Architecture

Support two primary logical asset types:

* PHOTO
* VIDEO

Use shared infrastructure where appropriate and format-specific processing where necessary.

A logical asset contains:

* Stable identity.
* Owner or rights holder.
* Gallery and event relationships.
* Title.
* Caption.
* Tags and keywords.
* AI analysis results.
* Matching and identification results.
* Metadata provenance.
* Available file variants.
* Storage ownership.
* Licence availability.
* Creator-defined prices.
* Purchase entitlements.

Avoid duplicating editorial metadata across variants of the same logical asset.

5.1 File/Object Model

Each persistent stored object should have:

* Owner.
* Parent asset.
* Object purpose.
* Storage provider.
* Storage key.
* MIME type.
* Actual byte size.
* SHA-256 checksum.
* Lifecycle state.
* Creation and deletion state.

Use existing storage abstractions where suitable.

Do not duplicate original files unnecessarily.

5.2 Photo Variants

A photo asset may contain:

* Primary JPEG.
* Optional RAW original.

5.3 Video Variants

A video asset may contain:

* Original video.
* Poster image.
* Playable preview.
* Representative analysis frames.
* Optional download variants.

Not every generated intermediate file must be retained permanently.

Use temporary processing and cleanup where possible.

6. JPEG and RAW Architecture

6.1 Mandatory JPEG

For the initial Marketplace version, every publishable photo asset must have at least one JPEG.

JPEG is the primary visual and AI-analysis representation.

RAW is an optional additional file variant.

Requirements:

* JPEG is required for publishing.
* RAW cannot be published or sold independently without an associated JPEG.
* JPEG and RAW share one logical media identity.
* Both originals remain byte-for-byte unchanged.
* The existing AI pipeline analyzes the JPEG.
* RAW does not trigger duplicate AI analysis.
* JPEG and RAW share tags, captions and matching results.
* RAW may have a different selling price.
* RAW counts toward the applicable storage allocation.

The JPEG requirement applies to both official VCO content and external Creator content.

6.2 RAW/JPEG Pairing

Support pairing suggestions based on:

* File stems.
* Compatible filenames.
* Capture timestamps when reliable.
* Camera metadata where available.
* Other trustworthy relationships.

Do not assume matching filenames always represent the same photograph.

Require Creator confirmation for ambiguous matches.

Support manual pairing and corrections.

Do not silently attach a RAW to the wrong JPEG.

6.3 RAW Downloads

* Preserve original RAW bytes.
* Support optional XMP sidecars.
* Preserve existing editing adjustments.
* Preserve unrelated XMP namespaces.
* Allow paired RAW/XMP delivery.
* Require explicit Creator permission for exact-original delivery.
* Warn about potentially sensitive embedded metadata.

Define supported RAW formats and size limits during technical planning.

7.2 AI Analysis Through Video Frames

Create a video-specific adapter around existing image-analysis components.

The adapter should:

1. Extract representative video frames.
2. Use bounded and configurable sampling.
3. Submit suitable frames to existing AI analysis.
4. Aggregate matching and tagging results.
5. Produce video-level captions.
6. Preserve evidence and confidence information.
7. Allow manual corrections.
8. Avoid unsupported identification claims.
9. Avoid repeated analysis of unchanged videos.
10. Enforce processing budgets.

Do not modify the existing photo AI pipeline merely to accommodate videos.

Frame extraction and AI processing should run asynchronously.

Avoid analyzing every video frame.

Choose sampling defaults based on measured quality, cost and performance.

8. Unified Storage Management

8.1 Separate Accounting, Shared Technology

Use the same storage infrastructure for official VCO and Creator media.

However, maintain separate accounting.

Official VCO

* No Creator subscription quota.
* No Creator quota-based upload blocking.
* No subscription required.
* Storage consumption measured internally.
* Processing and storage costs monitored.
* Technical safety limits still apply.

External Creator

* Subscription-based storage allowance.
* Optional purchased additional capacity.
* Uploads blocked when available quota is exhausted.
* All persistent Creator-owned objects count toward the quota.

8.2 Creator Quota Calculation

Creator allowance = active plan storage + active purchased storage grants.

Count:

* JPEG originals.
* RAW originals.
* Video originals.
* Persistent previews.
* Persistent derivatives.
* Creator-managed XMP sidecars.
* Archived and hidden assets.
* Creator-owned profile/reference objects.
* Upload reservations.
* Objects awaiting confirmed deletion.

Storage consumption does not depend on whether an asset is published or listed for sale.

Do not exclude archived media from storage accounting.

No special RAW tariff.

RAW simply consumes its actual stored capacity.

8.3 Storage Dashboard

Display:

* Total allowance.
* Used capacity.
* Available capacity.
* Usage by media type.
* Usage by gallery.
* Pending cleanup.
* Purchased storage grants.

Warn at approximately:

* 80%.
* 90%.
* 100%.

At 100%, block new Creator uploads safely.

Do not automatically delete existing content.

Release storage only after physical deletion is confirmed.

8.4 Large Uploads

The current R2 implementation has an approximately 100 MiB single-write limit.

Design reliable large-file uploads for RAW and video.

Evaluate:

* Multipart uploads.
* Direct-to-R2 transfers.
* Short-lived authorization.
* Server-side ownership validation.
* Quota reservations.
* Checksums.
* Retry and resume.
* Incomplete upload cleanup.
* Abuse prevention.

Prefer the simplest secure approach that avoids unnecessary server bandwidth and memory usage.

9. IPTC, EXIF and XMP Metadata

Professional metadata support must remain optional.

Uploads and publication must not require editorial IPTC/XMP completion.

Required legal rights declarations are separate.

9.1 Metadata Precedence

Use:

Explicit Creator edit → existing embedded metadata → eligible event/reference/AI information.

For official VCO content, authorized VCO editorial edits have the same precedence as Creator edits.

Preserve:

* Intentional blanks.
* Manual corrections.
* Suppressed AI values.
* Existing embedded Creator metadata.
* Field-level provenance.

Never silently overwrite manually entered information with AI.

9.2 Safe Metadata Inference

AI and reference data may suggest:

* Event.
* Series.
* Keywords.
* Captions.
* Confirmed driver identification.
* Confirmed vehicle identification.
* Relevant racing context.

Do not infer:

* Photographer identity from uploader identity.
* Copyright ownership from account identity.
* Licensing rights from AI.
* Depicted people solely from entry lists.
* Capture time from event schedules.
* GPS coordinates from circuit locations.

9.3 Metadata Standards

Use IPTC Core/Extension in XMP as the primary editorial representation.

Mirror compatible fields into IPTC IIM and EXIF where appropriate.

Reuse the installed ExifTool where suitable.

Support Unicode and structured metadata.

9.4 Download Metadata

* Preserve uploaded originals unchanged.
* Create metadata-enriched JPEG exports from working copies.
* Avoid recompression when changing metadata only.
* Preserve ICC profiles and orientation.
* Read back and validate generated metadata.
* Generate separate export checksums.
* Use non-destructive XMP sidecars for RAW.
* Preserve existing RAW editing settings.

9.5 Privacy

Standard exports should apply a tested privacy policy.

Review:

* GPS.
* Camera serial numbers.
* Contact information.
* MakerNotes.
* Embedded thumbnails.
* Hidden metadata namespaces.

Exact-original downloads may retain sensitive metadata.

Creators must explicitly enable exact-original delivery and receive appropriate warnings.
